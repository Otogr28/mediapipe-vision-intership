Physics you can pinch.

A camera on the display follows your bare hands as you move them. Touch your index finger to your thumb and the screen feels a click. With that one gesture you can grab, throw and steer eight small experiments, without ever touching anything.

Pinch Touch your index finger to your thumb. That is a click, and every button on the screen answers to it.
Pinch + hold Keep the pinch closed and you are holding whatever sits under your fingertips. Drag planets, stretch the slingshot, carry the cat.
Release Open your fingers to let go. From that moment on, whatever you were holding obeys the physics alone.

The eight experiments

Each one runs real physics, computed live while you play. Scan the QR code on any running experiment, or tap a card here, and you will find the story behind it.

Behind the screen

A small computer, an NVIDIA Jetson, watches the camera and finds 21 landmarks on each of your hands, thirty times a second, using a neural network. The distance between your fingertip and your thumb tells it when you have pinched. The physics itself, from orbits to ripples to curved spacetime, is computed live in the same little box and drawn over the camera image.

Nothing is recorded. The camera feed is processed inside the display and is never stored or sent anywhere. By the time you walk away, the frames are already gone.

Built during a Physics Department summer internship at Gordon College.
