Spacetime

Einstein's picture of gravity: mass does not pull, it bends. This is the famous stretched-sheet picture, drawn honestly and to scale.

At the display

  • Place a SUN, then a NEUTRON star, then a HOLE and compare their dents. The same idea makes wildly different depths, and that difference is the whole story.
  • Pinch ORBITER to send a marble around a mass, and try PRECESS to watch an oval orbit slowly swivel. That small swivel is the effect that made Einstein famous.
  • Pinch with both hands at once to grab the whole scene and spin it. The 2D and TOP buttons give you other angles.

What's really happening

Newton said that masses pull on each other, and left the question of how unanswered. Einstein's answer was that they do not reach across space at all. They reshape it. Mass curves spacetime, and everything moving through it simply follows the straightest path available. An orbit is not caused by a tug; an orbit is what going straight ahead looks like in a curved place.

The sheet is the standard way to picture that, but most versions cheat, drawing every dent as a dramatic funnel. This one does not. A star is not a point. It has a surface, so its dent is a smooth bowl that bottoms out. A black hole has no surface at all. Its funnel keeps going, steeper and steeper, until at the horizon the sheet runs vertical. What separates them is not mass but compactness: how much mass in how little size.

Here is the twist worth taking home. Far from either object, the two dents are identical. If the Sun were swapped tonight for a black hole of the same mass, Earth's orbit would not change at all. Black holes do not vacuum up galaxies. Up close they are extraordinary; from a distance they are just mass.

Worth knowing: Mercury's orbit swivels 43 arcseconds per century more than Newton could explain, a tiny error that nagged astronomers for decades. Einstein's curved spacetime predicted exactly that number, and he later wrote that the discovery gave him heart palpitations. PRECESS shows the same effect, exaggerated so you do not need a century.

KEEP EXPLORING: Black hole is this same curvature seen face-on through bent light, and Orbitals is the Newtonian version of the dance.
